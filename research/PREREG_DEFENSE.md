# Pre-registration: Defensive Matchup Engine, written before `research/defense_eval.py` was run on any data

Question: does knowing how the opposing defence, its individual defenders and the likely player-versus-defender matchup look add out-of-sample accuracy to the existing player projections? Nothing below changes production unless a feature group passes the rule at the end, and even then only after calibration and explicit approval. This is the same method and the same rule as `PREREG_HUMAN_FACTORS.md`; it does not create a second probability or EV path.

## What the data can and cannot support (inspected, not assumed)
| Wanted | Available? | How it is handled |
|---|---|---|
| Who guarded whom, per game, with matchup time, partial possessions, switches, shots faced, points, assists, turnovers, shooting fouls | **Yes**: NBA.com `BoxScoreMatchupsV3`, one request per game, pulled by `research/pull_matchups.py` into `research/data/matchups.sqlite` | per-GAME rows, so a feature for game N uses only games before N. Season-level matchup tables are NOT used: they already contain the games being predicted |
| Defended FG% by shot zone, rim protection (contests, rim FG% allowed), Synergy play types (pick-and-roll ball handler / roll man, isolation, post) | **Not point-in-time**: NBA.com only publishes these as season-to-date totals as of the day they are pulled, with no history | **not used in training** (using them would leak). Listed as unavailable; a daily snapshot job would be needed to accumulate them forward |
| Opponent shot location, shot quality | Not in the data | not used. Three-point attempts and free-throw rate allowed come from team box scores |
| Zone defence | No reliable source | not used |
| Starting lineups and injury report history | None historically | the opposing rotation is "who played in the team's last 10 games"; a player absent TONIGHT cannot be known historically, so cross-matching after a late absence is NOT modelled (listed as a limit) |
| Switches | The feed's `switchesOn` field is **zero in every one of 96,004 rows inspected** | not used; no switch rate feature exists. Switching and help defence are represented only indirectly, by the concentration of the estimated assignment weights (`mu_herf`) and by `help_fga` not being used |
| Offensive position | Listed only for starters (blank for about half the rows) | the guarded player's role class is G, F, C or U (U = no listed position, i.e. mostly bench players); U is a class like the others |
| Defender position | Not given (only the offensive player's position) | defenders are described by what they actually guarded (versatility = spread across offensive positions guarded), not by a listed position |
| Absolute matchup minutes | The `matchupMinutes` field does not sum to a player's real minutes (inspected: a starter's matchup minutes sum to about half of his box-score minutes) | matchup exposure is used only as a RELATIVE weight and as a sample-size count (`partialPossessions`), never as real minutes |

## Features (all prefix `df_`; strictly prior-only)
**TD: team defence** of the opponent, from team box scores: points allowed per 100 possessions adjusted for the strength of the offences faced (minus those offences' own prior average), 3PA allowed per 100 possessions, 3P% allowed, free-throw attempts allowed per FGA, opponent offensive-rebound share allowed, assists allowed per 100 possessions, turnovers forced per 100 possessions. Each over the last 10 team games and over the season to date, shrunk toward the league mean (computed from earlier games only) with weight n/(n+8) games (3P% and FT rate: 100 pseudo-attempts). Existing baseline features `opp_def`, `opp_pace`, `own_def` already contain raw points allowed, so these are measured as INCREMENTAL.
**PD: individual defenders** (the opposing rotation's quality, minutes-weighted, NOT an assignment): per defender, points over expected per 100 matchup possessions, where expected is the guarded player's own prior points per possession (so a defender is not credited or blamed for who he guarded), shrunk with weight poss/(poss+1500); shooting fouls per 100 possessions shrunk with +1500; 3PA faced per 100; blocks and steals per minute from player box scores (secondary, never alone); versatility (entropy of offensive positions guarded). Rotation weights are the players' minutes in the team's previous 10 games. A defender with little exposure is pulled to the league average, so 10 possessions never rates anyone elite or terrible.
**MU: likely matchup** for offensive player p against team T: weights w over T's rotation = `lambda * (p's own prior share of possessions against that defender) + (1 - lambda) * (share of T's rotation guarding p's role class)`, `lambda = n_direct/(n_direct+150)` possessions; role class is p's modal listed position in earlier matchup rows. Features: weighted defender quality (`mu_dpoe`), concentration of the weights (Herfindahl, a switch/mixing signal), weighted foul rate, and `mu_conf` = sum of weight x min(1, defender possessions/1500). **Matchup confidence** (descriptive, fixed now): High if `mu_conf` >= 0.6, Medium 0.3 to 0.6, Low below 0.3. If p has no earlier matchup rows the direct term is zero and the feature comes from the team role term alone; if that is unavailable too the value is missing (recorded).
**IX: interactions** (offensive skill x defensive weakness, only where a mechanism exists): `pts_l10 x mu_dpoe`, `fg3m_l10 x td_3pa_allowed`, `reb_l10 x td_oreb_share_allowed`, `ast_l10 x td_ast_allowed`, `pts_l10 x td_ftr_allowed`. No generic "weak defence" bonus is added.

**Units and constants.** `partialPossessions` is NBA.com's exposure measure, not a count of real possessions (a starter accumulates 50 to 80 per game). Shrinkage constants are in those units: 1,500 for defender rates (about 20 to 30 games of a starter), 800 for a guarded player's own expected rate, 150 for the direct-history weight. They were set after looking at the SCALE of that field on the first 449 games pulled, before any model was fitted, and are not tuned afterwards.

## Experiment (fixed now)
Variants per statistic (points, rebounds, assists, threes; PRA reported too, fitted on its own target): **A** baseline (production features); **B** + TD + team-based interactions; **C** + PD; **D** + MU + matchup interactions; **E** + everything (information; adopted only if every group in it individually passed).
Chronological walk-forward folds as before: A (train 2022-23, test 2023-24), B (train 2022-24, test 2024-25), F (train 2022-25, test 2025-26). Same rows for every variant, rotation players (last-10 minutes >= 20). Dispersion fitted once from the baseline on the last 20% of training. No season is split between train and test.
Reported: paired difference in negative-binomial log score with a 95% bootstrap interval resampling calendar weeks; Brier score, log loss and calibration slope/intercept on pseudo-lines (nearest half-integer to the last-10 average, NOT sportsbook lines); MAE; the paired difference by stat, by fold (season), by matchup-confidence bucket, by defender-sample-size bucket (`mu_conf` deciles of exposure), by role; partial association (Spearman of each feature with the baseline's standardised residual) and overlap (largest Spearman with any existing feature); feature coverage (missingness).

## Decision rule (fixed now)
A group is **kept as an experimental research feature** only if on BOTH development folds at least 3 of 4 statistics have an interval entirely above 0 and none entirely below, AND on the final season F none is entirely below 0. **F is used once, only for groups that passed A and B.** No bucket, stat or role subset can rescue a failed group. Even a kept group stays research-only until calibrated, pre-registered for production and approved; it may not alter the authoritative probability or any approval gate by default. The UI may show defensive FACTS with sample sizes whatever the result, but a probability impact is shown only for a validated, adopted group.

## Not claimed
Better accuracy against naive baselines is not an edge over sportsbooks: no price is involved. 2025-26 was seen by earlier experiments, so F is confirmation, not a pristine holdout. Matchup assignment is an estimate from history, not a confirmed assignment; switches, help defence and late absences blur it.
